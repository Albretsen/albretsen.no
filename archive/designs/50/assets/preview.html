02 / Palette

Infrared, on a void.

A false-colour thermal ramp from void through violet and magenta to orange, amber and white-hot vinyl. The seed hue (108°) is the phosphor yellow-green: the electric colour, kept for cursors, live signals and the easter egg. Use it sparingly.

03 / Type

Clean grotesk, pixel labels.

The pixel work is the costume, not the skeleton. Headlines and body text use Geist, a tight technical grotesk. Code and facts use Geist Mono. Silkscreen is only for micro labels, sticker print and UI chrome. All three are free on Google Fonts under the OFL.

04 / Identity

A sticker with its corner already lifting.

The mark is a 16×16 sprite: a violet die-cut tile with its top-right corner peeled back, and an A with a one-pixel magenta charge on its right edge. The wordmark is drawn on the same grid, with two-pixel stems and a magenta drop. Both are SVG rects, with crisp edges at any size.

05 / Hero imagery

Real vinyl, museum underdrawings.

The stills come from the same scene as the frames, rendered at 2× retina. The infrared plates are public-domain works from The Met, run through an 8×8 Bayer dither onto the infrared ramp and upscaled with nearest-neighbour, so every pixel stays a pixel.

06 / Products

The real logos, as stickers.

Each live or in-progress product's actual logo is printed, unmodified, on a die-cut vinyl sticker with its corner lifting off the liner. The facts on each label sticker come straight from content.js. Retired products get pixel stand-ins in the house style, except Luma, which still has its own logo.

07 / Stickers & extras

Things to scatter.

Twenty-one die-cut stickers (transparent WebP at 1× and 2×) for margins, footers, empty states and the 404 page. Hover one to see the LIFT move. The raw sprites also ship as a sprite sheet plus JSON maps, so you can draw them on a canvas or in 3D.

08 / Interactive & 3D

Sprites you can pick up.

Voxel GLBs built directly from the sprite maps, with a 2.5D height per colour and vertex colours, under 110 KB each. The viewer renders at 40% resolution and upscales with nearest-neighbour, so a live 3D object reads like a pre-rendered 16-bit sprite. The Lottie is drawn by hand from the same bolt sprite.

09 / Motion study

Four moves.

Every move is about a physical sticker, or about a screen from 1994. They live in motion.css and motion.js, and all of them respect reduced-motion. There is also a fifth, hidden one. It has to do with the hero verb.
