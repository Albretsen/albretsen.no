01 / Direction

Night side of the planet, one spark in the dark.

Photographs taken from the ISS at night show cities as sodium-orange networks under a blue horizon. They look like circuitry, which is the point: this is a technology company seen from orbit. The visitor holds a spark; each product is a lamp they light. Minimal futurist, no chrome: deep night blues, one warm light source, mono annotations, and particles that behave like molten metal.

02 / Identity

A thrown spark makes the A.

The mark is the parabola a spark draws when it is thrown, with the spark itself where the crossbar would be. The wordmark is monoline, round-capped, drawn on a 40-unit grid; its A is the same parabola.

03 / Palette

Night blues, one sodium lamp, and the pink it strikes with.

Seed hue 0° is the neon pink a low-pressure sodium lamp glows when it first strikes, before it warms to orange. It appears only in motion and in the cooling tail of particles. Sodium is the one warm accent at rest.

Molten ramp · filament → sodium → ember → neon (0°) → cooled night

05 / Products as lamps

The real logos, on glass, in orbit.

Each live or building product gets its own lamp: the untouched logo on a glass plinth, a sodium halo behind, a molten orbit around, and a different ISS night photograph underneath. Harbor is paused, so its lamp is dimmed to airglow blue.

07 / Object

The sodium orb.

Smoked glass with real fingerprint smudges (ambientCG) reflecting a real street lamp (Poly Haven HDRI), a filament inside, and 10,000 GPU particles in two molten rings. Drag to turn it, press to strike. three.js r170 loads only when this comes into view; the poster stands in until then.
